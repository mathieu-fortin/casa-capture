Privacy policy

Casa capture — effective 23 August 2026

Casa capture sends data to one address: the one you type into its options page, which is a server you host and operate. It has no other destination.

The author of this extension operates no server and receives no data from it — not now, not in aggregate, not anonymised. There is no analytics, no telemetry, no crash reporting and no third party of any kind. This is not a promise about how collected data is handled; it is that there is no collection to handle.

What leaves your browser

When you open a property page on a site the extension covers, it sends to your own Casa:

FieldWhat it is
urlThe address of the page you opened.
page_typeWhether it looked like a search page or a single listing.
documentsThe page's own machine-readable evidence, verbatim: its application/ld+json blocks and the JSON state blob its framework left in the DOM. Where a page publishes neither, a stripped copy of its main content instead.
droppedPresent only when something was too large or unreadable to include, so that "this page published nothing" can be told apart from "we discarded what it published".
captured_atWhen the capture was taken.
extension_versionThe extension's version.

Each request carries the token you configured, as an Authorization header, so your Casa can tell which of your installations sent it.

This includes the URLs of pages you visited

Stated plainly because it is easy to gloss over: the address of every page the extension captures leaves your browser. That is a subset of your browsing history. It is limited to the sites the extension runs on, it excludes the account pages described below, and it never includes a page you did not open yourself — but it is not nothing, and Firefox will tell you so on the install screen.

What never leaves your browser

  • Cookies and credentials. The extension does not read them.
  • Your account pages. Addresses matching the sites' signed-in areas — saved searches, alerts, favourites, messages, login and profile pages — are refused before anything on them is read.
  • Pages you did not open. Nothing here opens a page: not on a timer, not in a background tab, not while the browser is unattended. The queue of suggested pages is rendered as ordinary links, and the code contains no way to follow one.
  • Anything from a site you have not granted access to.

What is stored on your device

In the browser's extension storage, and nowhere else:

  • Your settings — the address of your Casa, your token, and any extra hosts you added.
  • A queue of captures waiting to be sent, if your Casa was unreachable. It holds at most 200, oldest dropped first, and a second capture of the same page replaces the first. An item leaves the queue when it is delivered.
  • The last outcome — whether the last send succeeded, and the error if it did not.

Uninstalling the extension removes all of it.

Permissions, and why each exists

PermissionWhy
storageThe queue and your settings, which have to survive the browser closing.
alarmsRetrying a send after your Casa has been unreachable. A background script is stopped when idle, so a timer would not survive; an alarm does.
scriptingRunning the collector on an extra site you add yourself, and only that.
Access to four property sitesThe sites it reads. Never <all_urls>.
Optional access to other addressesNothing is granted by this at install. Your Casa's LAN address cannot be written into an extension's manifest, so the extension asks for exactly that origin when you press Save — and for an extra site's origin if you add one.

What Firefox is told this collects

Firefox requires an add-on to declare what it transmits, and shows it to you before you install. Casa capture declares two things:

Website contentThe evidence blocks described above.
Browsing activityThe URLs of the pages it captures.

Both go only to the address you configured. Declaring them is a statement about what the software transmits, not about anyone receiving it other than you.

Children

This is a companion to self-hosted server software and is not directed at children. It collects nothing about who is using it — it has no account, no identifier and no profile.
